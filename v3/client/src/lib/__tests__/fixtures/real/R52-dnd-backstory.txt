# Robin Wood — backstory

*By Bella, as written on the D&D Beyond character sheet. Pasted 2026-09-22.*

Robin Woods grew up as a farm kid in a small farming community, where everyone knew everyone and life revolved around the land.

There was a small local temple nearby, and from a young age, she wanted to learn from the clerics there. More than anything, she wanted to help people. She believed that if she could learn the ways of the Earth Mother, she could use that knowledge to make life better for the people around her.

She learned quickly that helping people was often less about what you knew and more about what resources you had.

Medicine, supplies, spell components. Everything seemed to cost something. And the people who needed help the most were often the ones who couldn't afford it.

She knew some people her age who made their ends meet by stealing from travelers along the road. At first, she just started hanging around with them healing when they needed her. Then she realized she could use what they stole to get the supplies and components she needed to help people.

So she joined their merry band.

It seemed perfectly reasonable to her. Chauntea provided everything, didn't she? If something came from the earth, how could it truly belong to one person? And if someone had more than they needed while someone else was sick or starving, wasn't putting those resources to better use actually the right thing to do?

Eventually, they found out what she had been doing and told her she was no longer welcome there.

She was genuinely confused. She hadn't been stealing for herself. She had been stealing because she wanted to help people. If the supplies ultimately belonged to Chauntea, didn't using them to help others serve her purpose better than letting them sit in someone's pockets?

The local temple did not agree.

So she left town with her group of thief friends and began traveling with them, taking on mercenary work and doing whatever jobs they could find. Over time, though, the group slowly fell apart. One by one, her companions went their separate ways until she found herself traveling with an entirely new groups of people.

Now she travels as a cleric for hire.

She still believes that helping people matters more than rules about ownership. She still has a questionable relationship with the concept of "stealing." And she still doesn't quite understand why the temple was so upset with her.

But wherever she goes, if someone needs help and she has the means to provide it, she'll do what she can—whether she had to pay for those means or "redistribute" them along the way.
